About Daisen Soba

A Local Specialty: What Is Daisen Soba?

The roots of Daisen Soba reach back roughly 800 years, to the Kamakura period. Kikō Shōnin, a revered monk of Daisen Temple, is said to have performed a sacred rite before the Emperor while still wearing his straw cape and hat, healing the Emperor's illness. This episode was passed down as "the Secret Rite of the Straw Cape."

The monk also distributed amulets to protect cattle and horses, and made use of Mt. Daisen's vast foothills to encourage livestock grazing and buckwheat cultivation. These efforts became the origin of what would later be celebrated as the "Daisen Cattle and Horse Market," counted among Japan's three great livestock markets.

Since then, the tradition of soba-making has lived on at the foot of Mt. Daisen, with a distinctive milling technique that incorporates even the inner husk of the buckwheat. The deep, dark color and a rustic, robust flavor — this is "Daisen Soba," now arriving fresh at the table of your villa.
